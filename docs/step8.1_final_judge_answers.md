# Final Judge Answers — the 20 that matter

Selected from the 49 in `docs/step8.0_judge_qa.md`. Each has a **20–40 second spoken answer**, an optional
deeper answer if pressed, and the repository evidence.

**Answer discipline:** concede the point first where it is true, then show the measurement. Never defend a
claim the repository does not support.

---

### 1. Where is the demand–supply gap?

**Short (30s).** "We don't publish one, because we proved it isn't identifiable from the evidence that
exists. Supply by state and trade is a **joint distribution**. We hold its row totals, plus **21.8%** of its
column totals — 78% of PMKVY 4.0 enrolment sits in job roles the source doesn't name — and that fragment
comes from a *different scheme*. Row and column totals never determine the interior. That's a mathematical
result, not a backlog item."

**Deeper.** "Closing the system requires assuming `P(trade|state) = P(trade)` — that every state trains the
same trade mix. It's substantively false, since regional trade-mix variation is the very thing we're asked to
measure, and untestable, because not one state × trade cell exists to validate against. Independently, all
four subtraction forms fail a unit and time audit: demand is a cumulative stock of registrations at one date,
supply is candidates certified in a scheme window closing in 2020."

**Evidence.** `docs/step6_methodology_decision.md`; `GET /api/demand-supply-gap` → 200, `data: null`,
`reason_code: NOT_IDENTIFIABLE`, `blocking_gate: G-1`.

### 2. If you can't calculate the gap, how does this solve the problem statement?

**Short (35s).** "It doesn't solve it fully, and the requirement mapping says so — gap forecasting is
`NOT_SUPPORTED_YET`, not implemented. What we built is the platform such an engine requires: the
standardisation, the provenance, the evidence framework, the dashboard and API. And we produced something a
guessing system can't — a reproducible proof of which quantity is unidentifiable, and the exact dataset that
resolves it. A planner can request one dataset. They can't safely act on a fabricated gap, and they wouldn't
know it was fabricated."

**Deeper.** "Of ten PS requirements: six implemented, four partial, and the three depending on a gap are
blocked by evidence rather than engineering. Standardisation, the dashboard, the API, the methodology
documentation and the pilot scope requirement are all genuinely met."

**Evidence.** `docs/step8.0_requirement_mapping.md`.

### 3. Where is the AI? The PS says "AI-Enabled."

**Short (35s).** "I'll be direct: **there's no machine-learning model in production, and no ML library is
installed.** We won't invent one to match a title. The reason is substantive — every ML capability this PS
implies is blocked by the same missing evidence, not by modelling. Forecasting needs two dated demand
observations; we have one. A learned gap model needs a target variable; the target isn't identifiable. An
early-warning classifier needs labels; no ground-truth shortage label exists."

**Deeper.** "What the system does is data engineering and statistical reasoning: acquisition under a licence
gate, PDF and HTML extraction, taxonomy and geography conformance, contract validation, deterministic signal
construction, weakest-link confidence. Training a model on data of this shape would produce confident output
with no validity — the exact failure the rest of the submission avoids."

**Evidence.** `pyproject.toml` — no scikit-learn, LightGBM, XGBoost, PyTorch, TensorFlow, statsmodels or
statsforecast; no model code in `src/` or `api/`.

---

### 4. Why is there no ML model? What would it be?

**Short (30s).** "Because the inputs for a defensible model don't exist yet. When they do, it's specified,
not hand-waved: mandatory naive baselines first, then classical per-series models where history permits, then
a pooled global gradient-boosted model across districts — many short series is exactly where pooling wins —
with rolling-origin backtesting and MASE, interval coverage and top-N rank overlap."

**Deeper.** "Deep learning is rejected on evidence, not fashion: insufficient history, no interpretability,
nothing a planner can defend in a budget discussion. All of it waits on gate G-4 — a second dated NCS
observation."

**Evidence.** `docs/step8.0_requirement_mapping.md` §3 U5; Step 0 §11.4.

### 5. Why not use synthetic data to demonstrate the full pipeline?

**Short (30s).** "We considered it and rejected it. A synthetic gap looks exactly like a real one — the error
would be invisible, systematically wrong in one direction, and these outputs inform seat sanctioning, which
is real money. Our own rules would have required any synthetic row to be generated by committed seeded code,
flagged at row level, visibly marked in every UI surface and export, and documented as a limitation. **We
used none.**"

**Deeper.** "The deeper reason: a synthetic demonstration proves the pipeline runs, which was never in doubt.
It proves nothing about whether the quantity is knowable — and that was the actual open question."

**Evidence.** No synthetic row exists; verified across all 40 tables.

### 6. Isn't your district ranking just enterprise density?

**Short (30s).** "**Within a state, yes — and we print that above the table.** We measured it: in zero of
thirty-six states does the within-state ordering differ from ranking districts by Udyam enterprise share. The
NCS demand component only varies *between* states. The caveat is stored as a column on every row, and the
publication contract refuses to build a response if it's missing."

**Deeper.** "We also decomposed the district × occupation signal: district effect 61.2%, occupation effect
30.7%, genuinely district-specific occupational information **8.1%**. We publish that too. And it's why the
occupation view promotes the one comparison that restates neither input — districts within a state and NCO
division, which differs from both inputs in 27 of 27 groups tested."

**Evidence.** `demand_district_relative_signal.within_state_ranking_caveat`;
`docs/step7.3_dashboard_api_product_design.md` §1.

### 7. Why is Census 2011 acceptable in a 2024 analysis?

**Short (25s).** "It's the most recent Indian census occupational structure that exists — 2021 wasn't
conducted. We use it as a **structural prior only**, never as a demand measure: every row carries
`not_a_demand_measure = TRUE`. And the fourteen-year gap is disclosed on the face of the product — the
district page literally says *'Census occupation structure is 2011; the demand baseline is 2024-11-15.'*"

**Deeper.** "It's also the single largest reason output C is **LOW** confidence, and that confidence is
weakest-link, so nothing can lift it above LOW except better data. Our unlock roadmap names the alternative:
a PLFS district occupation table, or NCO-coded NCS vacancies, which would remove the Census dependency
entirely."

**Evidence.** `analytical_district_occupation_structure`; `demand_district_occupation_signal`
(`overall_confidence = LOW`, all rows).

### 8. Why is your data old — November 2024?

**Short (25s).** "2024-11-15 is the as-on date of the most recent NCS figures **published in Parliament
answers** — it's the frontier of what's officially available, not a stale choice. The dashboard shows that
date on every relevant figure and never says 'current' or 'live'. If NCS published monthly machine-readable
district data, our ingestion layer would take it unchanged."

**Deeper.** "There are five vintages on the surface — NCS 2024-11-15, Udyam 2023-12-21, PMKK 2024-03-31, PLFS
April 2025, Census 2011 — and we deliberately show **no global 'data as of' date**, because any single date
would be false. That's enforced: 'data as of' and 'live' are prohibited strings in our terminology lint."

**Evidence.** `vintage_policy` in `config/publication_contract.yaml`; the Methodology page vintage table.

### 9. What exactly is estimated, and what is observed?

**Short (30s).** "Observed: NCS state demand and NCS demand by industry — published counts traceable to three
named Parliament answers. Estimated: the national occupation composition, and the two district allocation
signals. Supporting: PLFS, which is observed but explicitly not a demand measure. And the distinction isn't
a label we type — **evidence status is read from each table's own column**, never declared in config."

**Deeper.** "We have a test that forges a config claiming a derived output is OBSERVED, and proves the API
still reports ESTIMATED. That test exists because an earlier step's own summary made exactly that mistake,
and we wanted it to be structurally impossible."

**Evidence.** `src/lmis/publish/contract.py`;
`tests/test_publication_contract.py::test_evidence_status_is_read_from_the_data_not_the_contract`.

---

### 10. How do you know your estimates are reliable?

**Short (30s).** "We don't claim they're reliable — we claim they're **labelled**. Output B is MEDIUM, output
C is **LOW**, and LOW by construction rather than by tuning. Confidence is weakest-link: the *worst* of
source quality, allocation depth, occupation mapping, temporal compatibility, geography coverage and
missingness. Never an average, because averaging lets a strong term mask a fatal one."

**Deeper.** "What we *can* demonstrate is correctness of construction: 14 of 14 reconciliation checks match
each source's own published totals exactly, and 479 tests pin the behaviour. There's no ground-truth
shortage label anywhere, so predictive validation is impossible and we don't claim it — the appropriate
framework is methodological validation plus sensitivity analysis, which is what we did."

**Evidence.** `src/lmis/demand/confidence.py`; `fact_training_reconciliation`; `fact_data_quality`.

### 11. What happens when a district has no Census mapping?

**Short (25s).** "It returns `NOT_AVAILABLE` with the actual reason — never a zero, never a blank. And the
reasons are distinguished: 522 districts belong to states whose B-24 table we haven't acquired, while **125
are post-2011 districts for which a Census prior can never exist.** Different problems, reported
differently."

**Deeper.** "**No row in the system carries a signal of zero** — verified by test. A district without a prior
has no occupation rows at all, just one placeholder with a NULL division, so a zero can't be rendered even by
accident. The UI shows a status panel, not an empty table."

**Evidence.** `demand_district_occupation_signal.occupation_prior_status`;
`tests/test_api.py::test_missing_census_returns_not_available_with_a_reason`.

### 12. What happens to the 58.13% PAN-India vacancies?

**Short (25s).** "Nothing — deliberately. **20,507,320 of 35,275,833 published NCS vacancies are PAN-India or
multiple-state** and are attributable to no state. They stay on a NATIONAL row, are disclosed on every
relevant screen, and **allocating them is a `PROHIBITED` capability** with its own blocked API route. If we
distributed them, every district figure would be inflated by an invented geography."

**Deeper.** "It also means the district signal rests on **41.87%** — 14,768,513 vacancies — and we state that
wherever a district figure appears. In the state list the residual is excluded by default, because it would
otherwise rank first, at 5.2× the largest actual state."

**Evidence.** `demand_coverage_summary`; `GET /api/residual-allocation` → `reason_code: PROHIBITED`.

### 13. Can the system tell MSDE which trade to train more?

**Short (25s).** "**No.** That requires occupation-level supply, which doesn't exist at any geographic grain —
and 153 of our 155 catalogued trades have no official NCO mapping. What the system *can* do is show which
district × occupation combinations carry a higher relative allocation signal within a state, at LOW
confidence over 138 districts — and tell MSDE precisely which dataset would let it answer the trade question
properly."

**Evidence.** `dim_trade_mapping_status`; `GET /api/supply/state-occupation` → `NOT_IDENTIFIABLE`.

### 14. What data would unlock the actual gap?

**Short (30s).** "One thing above all: **state-by-trade — ideally district-by-trade — training outcomes with
complete trade marginals, not a Top-N subset.** That's gate G-1 condition six: one observed interior cell of
supply by geography and trade. The other five conditions — same time grain, geography, occupation grain, unit,
population definition — are harmonisation problems that better data solves. Condition six is the
identification barrier, and **more of the same marginals never passes it.**"

**Deeper.** "Then, in order: the remaining 153 official trade-to-NCO mappings; Census B-24 for the other 33
states, which takes district coverage from 138 toward 785 **with no formula change**; a second dated NCS
snapshot for any forecast; and NCO-coded NCS vacancies, which would remove the Census prior entirely."

**Evidence.** `docs/step8.0_requirement_mapping.md` §3; `GET /api/capabilities`.

### 15. Why not scrape Naukri or Indeed?

**Short (25s).** "Three reasons, in order. **Terms of service** — it would disqualify a government-facing
tool. **Postings aren't vacancies** — re-posted, duplicated across platforms, structurally biased toward
white-collar urban roles while most blue-collar hiring never appears online. And it **wouldn't fix our actual
blocker**, which is supply-side. We'd still have no state × trade supply, so still no gap."

**Evidence.** `config/sources.yaml` licence gate; Step 0 risk register R-05.

### 16. How do you prevent hallucinations?

**Short (30s).** "Structurally, not by prompting. **No model generates any value anywhere in the pipeline.**
Every number traces to a named source document. Fuzzy, semantic, embedding and LLM-generated mappings are
prohibited project-wide — which is exactly why 153 of 155 trades remain unmapped rather than matched by name
resemblance."

**Deeper.** "Four enforcement layers: the contract refuses to **load** if a mode is weakened; the app refuses
to **start** if a label or translation string fails the terminology lint or any output claims a shortage;
every **response** carries its evidence envelope; every **export** carries it as a file header. Plus
`is_measured_shortage = false` by database constraint and regression test."

**Evidence.** `src/lmis/publish/terminology.py`; `api/main.py::verify_contract`.

### 17. What is technically innovative here?

**Short (35s).** "Not the FastAPI or the dashboard. Four things. **Identification-aware analytics** — we
distinguish 'we lack this data' from 'this quantity isn't identifiable from evidence of this shape', and treat
the second as a mathematical result. **A publication contract enforced in code**, with startup refusal on
breach. **A terminology lint over the published surface**, translation files included, with negation detection
so the product can still say 'this is *not* a vacancy count'. And **caveats stored as data** — our two signals
carry their own derivation caveats as columns, so the UI can't drift from the data."

**Deeper.** "The one I'd defend hardest is the arithmetic self-criticism: we measured that our own district
ordering is the Udyam ordering within a state, and published it above the table instead of hiding it."

**Evidence.** `config/publication_contract.yaml`; `src/lmis/publish/`;
`docs/step8.1_innovation_slide.md`.

### 18. How does the system scale?

**Short (25s).** "On the dimension that matters — evidence, not rows. Adding a source means a registry entry,
one conformer to the existing masters, and a Pandera contract. Nothing downstream changes shape. Concretely:
**Census B-24 for the remaining 33 states takes output C from 138 districts toward 785 with no formula
change** — the estimator already handles the full grid and reports per-district status."

**Deeper.** "82,358 rows query in milliseconds on DuckDB over Parquet, and would handle two orders more. The
serving layer is stateless and read-only. Median API response is about 14 milliseconds across 15 routes."

**Evidence.** `docs/step7.5_sih_demo_readiness.md` §1 item 19.

### 19. What happens if better government data becomes available?

**Short (25s).** "The architecture absorbs it without redesign, because the conformance layer, estimator
scaffolding, confidence framework, publication contract and serving layer already exist. The work would be
acquisition and validation, not architecture. That's precisely why we spent this project building the
platform rather than a number."

**Deeper.** "And it's gated, not automatic. Nine objective gates govern promotion, each requiring recorded
evidence and an ADR. No gate may be weakened to admit an output — weakening a gate is itself a decision
requiring its own ADR."

**Evidence.** `docs/step7.1_indicator_publication_decision.md` §4 (gates G-1…G-9).

### 20. What is the weakest part of your solution?

**Short (30s).** "Output C — the district × occupation signal. It covers 138 of 785 districts, rests on a
fourteen-year-old occupational structure, and within a district its ordering is simply the Census ordering. We
print all three facts above the table rather than in a footnote. Second weakest: we have no map — it was
designed and not built, which we record as partial rather than claiming it."

**Deeper.** "A third, honestly: our accessibility was verified structurally through a DOM harness rather than
with a real screen reader. We'd want a manual pass before any real deployment. And Hindi is 21 of 106 keys —
it falls back to English rather than machine-translating official occupational terminology, because a wrong
official term is worse than an untranslated one."

**Evidence.** `docs/step7.5_sih_demo_readiness.md` §4.
