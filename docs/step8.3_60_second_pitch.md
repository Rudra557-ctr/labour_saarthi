# 60-Second Pitch

For a corridor conversation, a judge arriving late, or a "tell me about your project" opener.
**No forecasting or gap capability is claimed.** ≈ 185 words.

---

## The pitch

> **What problem?**
> "India sets skilling targets using labour-market data spread across six official sources that can't be
> joined — five vintages spanning fourteen years, two occupation classifications, and not one source
> carrying a district-by-occupation vacancy count.
>
> **What did we build?**
> LMIS. Twenty-six official sources acquired under a licence gate into an immutable checksummed store, then
> conformed onto NCO-2015, LGD geography and NIC sections through official concordances — and published so
> that every number carries its evidence status, confidence, coverage, vintage and source document. Thirty
> API routes, an accessible bilingual dashboard, four hundred and seventy-nine tests, and one command
> rebuilds everything from raw bytes.
>
> **What's technically different?**
> The problem statement asks for a demand–supply gap. We proved it isn't identifiable — supply by state and
> trade is a joint distribution and we hold only its margins. So rather than publish a number that would
> restate an assumption, the system publishes the finding, and the one dataset that would change it.
>
> **Why does it matter?**
> A planner can act on a specific data request. They can't act safely on a fabricated gap — and they
> wouldn't know it was fabricated."

---

## Delivery

**Pace:** unhurried — this is 60 seconds of content, not 45 compressed.
**Emphasis:** land *"not one source carries a district-by-occupation vacancy count"* and *"they wouldn't
know it was fabricated."* Those two lines carry the pitch.
**Pause** for a beat before "What's technically different?" — it's the turn in the argument.

## If they have only 20 seconds

> "LMIS standardises six fragmented official labour-market sources onto one occupation and one geography
> spine, and publishes demand intelligence where every number carries its evidence and coverage. And where
> the problem statement asks for a demand–supply gap, we proved it isn't identifiable from the data that
> exists — so we publish that, plus the one dataset that would unlock it."

## Never say in any version

"forecast" · "predict" · "skill gap" (except negated) · "shortage" · "AI-powered" · "supply" for training
outcomes · "demand ranking" · "real-time" · any impact number.
