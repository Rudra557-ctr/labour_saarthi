# Step 3.1 — Estimation Methodology & Evidence Resolution

Date: 2026-10-04 · **Design and evidence resolution only.** No estimator, no demand index, no district
demand, no forecasts, no gap scores, no recommendations, no API/frontend/LLM, no synthetic data.
Nothing bypassed: no registration, authentication, data-use agreement, TLS control or CAPTCHA.

---

## Headline

**The blocking question is resolved.** A real, official industry × occupation cross-tabulation for India
exists and has been acquired: **ILOSTAT indicator `EMP_TEMP_ECO_OCU_NB_A`**, whose India source code
`BA:14121` resolves to **"LFS – Periodic Labour Force Survey"**. It is India's own PLFS, harmonised and
published by the ILO as a cross-tab MoSPI does not publish directly.

It gives `P(occupation | industry)` at **NIC-2008 section × NCO-2015 division**, for 2022–2025.
It does **not** give a state dimension, so `P(occupation | industry, state)` remains unavailable and the
methodology below is explicit that the bridge is **national**.

With that, Step 4 is implementable from observed data alone. One term in the originally proposed formula
turns out to be **not identifiable** and is removed — see §9.

## 2. Industry → occupation bridge — **RESOLVED (national)**

### What was found

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Resource | ILOSTAT indicator `EMP_TEMP_ECO_OCU_NB_A` — "Employment by economic activity and occupation (thousands)" |
| Access | Documented public API, `https://rplumber.ilo.org/data/indicator/?id=…&ref_area=IND&format=.csv`; OpenAPI spec at `/openapi.json` |
| Underlying survey | **`BA:14121` = "LFS – Periodic Labour Force Survey"** (resolved from ILOSTAT's own source dictionary) |
| Fields | `ref_area, source, indicator, classif1, classif2, time, obs_value, obs_status, note_*` |
| `classif1` | economic activity — **ISIC-Rev.4 section** (`ECO_ISIC4_A` … `ECO_ISIC4_T`) |
| `classif2` | occupation — **ISCO-08 major group** (`OCU_ISCO08_1` … `_9`) |
| Units | **thousands of persons** (employment stock) |
| Geography | **IND only — no state dimension** |
| Vintage | 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 at this granularity; catalogue last updated 2026-10-02 |
| Scope | 20 sections × 9 major groups; 715 usable rows; **45 cells absent → kept NULL** |
| Licence | ILO terms of use; **licence_verified: false** — must be confirmed before publication |

### Why it legitimately bridges, without inventing anything

Two documented correspondences, both verified against primary sources earlier in this project:

- **ISIC-Rev.4 section → NIC-2008 section.** NIC-2008 is the Indian adaptation of ISIC-Rev.4; its section
  letters and titles were extracted from the official NIC-2008 PDF in Step 3.0 (21 sections, A–U).
- **ISCO-08 major group → NCO-2015 division.** NCVET's *Report on Mapping of Qualifications with NCO Codes*
  (22 Aug 2023), Annexure IX §3: *"There is one to one correspondence between ISCO – 08 and the NCO – 2015"*,
  with the first digit representing the Division / Major Group.

So the cross-tab is already in our taxonomy. **No manual mapping was created, no percentages were
generated by a model, and no expert judgement was substituted for data.**

### Evidence that it behaves correctly

Conditional distributions sum to 1 within every section in all four years. Face validity (2025):

| NIC section | Modal NCO-2015 division | Share |
|---|---|---|
| C Manufacturing | 7 Craft and related trades | 52.2% |
| F Construction | 9 Elementary occupations | 66.7% |
| J Information & communication | 2 Professionals | 61.9% |
| G Wholesale & retail | 5 Service and sales | 74.3% |
| Q Human health & social work | 3 Technicians (34.6%) / 2 Professionals (34.5%) | — |

**Temporal stability (2022 → 2025), 179 matched cells:** mean |ΔP| = **0.017**, median = **0.007**,
max = 0.188. Instability is concentrated in small sections (L real estate, R arts). This is the empirical
basis for treating the bridge as time-stable in the near term.

### Residual limitation

`P(occupation | industry, state)` is **NOT available**. Using the national bridge for every state assumes
the occupational composition of an industry does not vary by state — which is **false in degree** (a
Maharashtra manufacturing plant mix differs from Uttar Pradesh's). This is the single largest assumption in
the Step 4 methodology and must be labelled on every output. The only legitimate upgrade path is PLFS
unit-level microdata.

**PLFS unit-level microdata — PENDING.** `microdata.gov.in` presents a certificate chain containing a
**self-signed certificate** (openssl verify error 19), i.e. a privately-rooted chain I will not add trust
for, and unit-level access additionally requires registration and a data-use agreement. Not acquired.

## 3. PAN-India residual — **DECISION: B + C**

No official DGE documentation was found that attaches any further geography to
"Multiple States/PAN India". The category means what it says: the employer posted for multiple states or
nationwide. It is **a real attribute of the vacancy, not missing data**.

**Baseline treatment: keep it as a separate `NATIONAL` row and exclude it from district allocation
(option B), with option C available as a clearly labelled sensitivity scenario.** Option A (exclude and
forget) is rejected because it would hide 58.1% of the measure.

**Dashboard communication requirement.** Any screen showing district or state demand must state, adjacent
to the number, that it is built on the **41.9% of NCS vacancies that are state-attributable**, and show the
residual as its own national figure (20,507,320). "Coverage: 41.9% of vacancies are geographically
attributable" is a required caption, not a footnote.

## 4. Census 2011 occupation prior — **DECISION: A (hold district shares constant), with a published check**

Considered: hold constant (A), state-level calibration (B), or another method (C).

**Recommendation: A — hold the district-level Census-2011 occupation *shares* constant**, and publish a
comparison against the ILO national distribution as a *diagnostic*, not an adjustment.

Reasoning:

- A state-level calibration (B) would need a current **state × occupation** distribution. We do not have
  one: the ILO cross-tab is national, and PLFS monthly bulletins are national. Calibrating district shares
  to a *national* distribution would impose national structure on districts, destroying the only
  district-level occupational signal we have — which is the entire reason for using Census.
- Shares are far more stable over time than counts, so holding shares constant is the weaker, safer
  assumption.
- The instability is knowable and should be *reported*: Census-2011 NCO-2004 division shares can be
  compared with the ILO 2025 NCO-2015 division distribution at national level, and the divergence published
  as a freshness warning per division.

**Not done here:** no Census data was adjusted, and no synthetic district shares were created.

## 5. NCO-2004 → NCO-2015 confidence policy — **DECISION: division level, purity as confidence, no exclusion**

The threshold question was answered with measurement rather than a chosen number.

**Finding: going finer makes the mapping worse.**

| Mapping level | Source codes | Weighted purity | Min purity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Division (1-digit)** | 9 | **0.887** | **0.789** |
| Sub-division (2-digit) | 27 | 0.718 | 0.358 |

Sub-division mapping is therefore **rejected on evidence** — it roughly doubles the error and 2 of 27
sub-divisions rest on fewer than 10 concorded codes.

**Low purity is not confined to small divisions**, so excluding low-purity divisions is also rejected:

| NCO-2004 division | Purity | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| 3 Technicians | 0.789 | 303 |
| 8 Plant & machine operators | 0.805 | **727 — the largest** |
| 9 Elementary | 0.880 | 100 |
| 7 Craft | 0.903 | 681 |
| 2 Professionals | 0.998 | 428 |

Division 8 alone carries 727 concorded codes; dropping it would remove a large share of exactly the
blue-collar occupations the project exists to analyse. Division 3's leakage is explainable, not random:
239 → division 3, **60 → division 2**, 4 → division 7 — the ISCO-88→ISCO-08 reclassification that promoted
some technicians to professionals.

**Policy for Step 4:**

1. Use **division-level** mapping only. Do not map at sub-division level.
2. Carry measured purity as `division_map_confidence` on every row (already implemented in Step 3.0).
3. **Do not exclude** low-purity divisions; instead, propagate their purity into the output confidence so
   a weak link caps the result (§7).
4. Retain `alternative_divisions` so a consumer can see where the remainder goes.
5. Division `X` (workers not classified) stays **unmapped** — never forced into a division.

## 7. Uncertainty methodology — designed, not implemented

**Principle: communicate uncertainty as uncertainty.** No invented numeric probabilities. A single number
like "83% confidence" would have no statistical basis and is forbidden.

Each estimate carries seven recorded dimensions, each with a defined, auditable value:

| Dimension | Values | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `source_confidence` | OBSERVED / SURVEY_ESTIMATE / ADMINISTRATIVE | what the source is |
| `mapping_confidence` | measured purity (0–1) | NCO division crosswalk; NCS→NIC alignment |
| `temporal_freshness` | CURRENT (≤1 yr) / RECENT (1–3 yr) / STALE (>3 yr) / HISTORICAL (>10 yr) | source vintage vs reference period |
| `geographic_support` | DIRECT / ALLOCATED / NATIONAL_PROXY | was the geography observed, derived, or assumed from national? |
| `occupation_support` | DIRECT / DIVISION_MAPPED / NOT_AVAILABLE | how the occupation was obtained |
| `statistical_support` | CENSUS / SURVEY_WITH_N / SURVEY_WITHOUT_N / NONE | sample backing, where published |
| `transformation_depth` | integer — count of multiplicative derivations | 0 = observed, 3 = three layers |

**Overall confidence is the weakest link, not an average** — averaging lets a strong term mask a fatal one:

```
overall_confidence =
  LOW     if any dimension is HISTORICAL, NATIONAL_PROXY, NOT_AVAILABLE,
             mapping_confidence < 0.85, or transformation_depth >= 3
  MEDIUM  if any dimension is STALE or ALLOCATED, or mapping_confidence < 0.95
  HIGH    only if every dimension is DIRECT/CURRENT and transformation_depth <= 1
```

**Consequence, stated plainly: under this rule every district × occupation estimate Step 4 can currently
produce will be LOW confidence** — because the occupation prior is HISTORICAL (2011), the bridge is a
NATIONAL_PROXY, the geography is ALLOCATED, and transformation depth is 3. That is the correct and honest
result. The framework is not calibrated to produce flattering output.

## 8. What the future estimator may and may not claim

| Status | Definition | Allowed when | Example wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OBSERVED** | directly measured and published by the source, at the grain shown | the number appears in the source at that grain | "Tamil Nadu: 1,715,285 vacancies mobilised (NCS, cumulative since inception, as on 2024-11-15)" |
| **SUPPORTING** | real observed data used as structure or context, not as the quantity of interest | Udyam shares, Census shares, PLFS rates | "Pune holds 24% of Maharashtra's registered enterprises (Udyam, 2023)" |
| **ESTIMATED** | derived by a documented transformation from observed inputs | every district × occupation output | "Estimated demand signal (relative): rank 3 of 36 districts" |
| **NOT_AVAILABLE** | no evidence exists at this grain | 11 pilot districts without a Census prior | "No occupation breakdown available for this district" |

**Hard prohibitions for Step 4:**

- **Never** "District X has 4,500 vacancies" unless 4,500 is observed at district level. It is not, and no
  current source makes it so.
- The district × occupation output is a **relative demand signal for ranking**, not an absolute count. It
  must be published as a rank, a share, or an index — never as a number of jobs.
- No output may omit its `observation_status` or `overall_confidence`.
- `ESTIMATED` may never be rendered in the same visual style as `OBSERVED`.

## 9. Is the proposed estimator identifiable? — **One term must be removed**

The formula proposed in Step 1.6 was:

```
estimated_demand[district, occupation, period]
  = observed_state_industry_demand[state, industry, period]     (i)
  × district_industry_weight[district, industry]                (ii)
  × occupation_given_industry[state, occupation, industry]       (iii)
```

Term-by-term assessment against what actually exists:

| Term | Status | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| (i) state × industry demand | **NOT OBSERVED** | NCS publishes state and industry as **separate marginal tables**. No joint state × industry cross-tab exists, and deriving one would assume independence. |
| (ii) district × industry weight | **NOT OBSERVED** | Udyam gives district × **size class**, not district × NIC section. There is no industry dimension in it. |
| (iii) P(occupation \| industry, state) | **NATIONAL ONLY** | ILO bridge has no state dimension. |

**So the formula as written is not identifiable: two of its three terms do not exist.** Implementing it
would require inventing a state × industry split and a district × industry split. Both are forbidden.

### Proposed replacement — identifiable from observed data

Decompose into two separately-published quantities rather than one false product:

**(A) National occupation demand composition — fully derivable, no allocation**

```
national_occupation_signal[d] = Σ_s  V_national[s] × P(d | s)
```
where `V_national[s]` is observed NCS vacancies for NIC section `s` (national, from
`analytical_demand_by_industry`) and `P(d | s)` is the ILO conditional distribution. Both terms observed;
**no geographic allocation at all**; transformation depth 1. This is the strongest honest output available
and should be Step 4's primary deliverable.

**(B) District relative demand signal — one allocation layer, clearly labelled**

```
district_signal[g] = V_state_attributable[state(g)] × enterprise_share_within_state[g]
```
Both terms observed (NCS state vacancies; Udyam district shares). It carries **no occupation dimension**,
because no district × industry evidence exists to carry one. Transformation depth 1.

**(C) District × occupation — only as an explicitly compound, LOW-confidence ranking**

```
district_occupation_signal[g, d] = district_signal[g] × occupation_share_of_district[g, d]
```
using the Census-2011 district occupation share, **not** the ILO bridge — because the Census term is the
only district-level occupational evidence that exists. Transformation depth 2, occupation support
HISTORICAL, so overall confidence **LOW**. Publish as rank only, for the 138 districts that have a prior.

**Normalisation and denominators:** (A) sums over NIC sections and needs no normalisation. (B)'s weights
sum to 1 within state by construction (tested). (C) is normalised within district. Industries **are**
summed in (A) — legitimate because sections are mutually exclusive — but the `Sector Not Specified` row and
any section whose mapping confidence is below 0.9 must be reported separately, not folded in silently.

**Why (A) and (C) are kept separate:** multiplying the ILO national bridge by Census district shares would
apply two different occupational structures to the same quantity and double-count the occupational signal.

## 10. Temporal problem — **DECISION: cross-sectional baseline, explicitly labelled**

NCS has exactly one usable as-on date (2024-11-15, corroborated by three documents). Therefore Step 4 must
produce a **single cross-sectional snapshot labelled `BASELINE_2024-11-15`**, not a time series.

Prohibited: fabricating monthly demand, interpolating NCS vacancies, or constructing any time series from
one observation. `flow_available = False` with its stated reason is already enforced and tested.

**Before forecasting can begin, all of the following are required:**

1. **A second NCS as-on date** at least one quarter apart, with an identical measure definition verified
   before differencing. One pair gives one flow — a minimum of 4–6 dated observations is needed before any
   trend claim.
2. More PLFS monthly bulletins (one per month is published) for a context time series.
3. Dated training-supply data for the supply side.

Note the structural problem even then: differencing a *cumulative* series gives a flow, but the PAN-India
residual must be differenced separately, and any change in NCS portal coverage between dates is
indistinguishable from a change in labour demand. This must be stated whenever a flow is published.

**Strongest legitimate target, in order:**

1. **PMKVY district-wise enrolled/trained/assessed/certified/placed** (data.gov.in). The resource page

Even at best, the vintage is 2022 and the coding is scheme-training-type, **not** NCO — so a
trade → NCO mapping will be its own evidence problem.

## 13. Answer to the stop condition

> *"Exactly how will Step 4 estimate district-level occupation demand using only defensible real-data
> transformations?"*

**It will not produce a single district × occupation demand number. It will publish three separate
quantities, each with its own evidence chain:**

- **(A)** national occupation demand composition — observed NCS industry vacancies × observed ILO/PLFS
  conditional occupation distribution. Depth 1. **No allocation.** The flagship output.
- **(B)** district relative demand signal — observed NCS state vacancies × observed Udyam district
  enterprise shares. Depth 1. **No occupation dimension.**
- **(C)** district × occupation ranking — (B) × Census-2011 district occupation shares, for the **138 of 149**
  pilot districts with a prior. Depth 2. **LOW confidence, published as rank only.**

**Missing datasets that would materially improve this, named explicitly:** PLFS unit-level microdata (for a
state-level bridge); a second dated NCS observation (for any flow); PMKVY district training data (for the
supply side); an authoritative district parent→child mapping (for the 11 districts).

**None of these gaps is filled with synthetic or invented data.**
