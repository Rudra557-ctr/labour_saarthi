# Value Proposition — LMIS · PS 26246

All claims verified against the repository. **No forecasting or gap capability is implied anywhere below.**

---

## One-line tagline (primary)

> **Evidence-first labour-market intelligence — it publishes what the data supports, and names what is
> missing.**

### Three alternatives

1. **Standardised official labour-market evidence, with provenance on every number.**
   *(Most literal. Best for a written abstract or a one-line submission field.)*
2. **Demand intelligence that shows its evidence — and refuses to invent the rest.**
   *(Most memorable. Best spoken, as a deck subtitle or an opening line.)*
3. **From fragmented government data to defensible skilling intelligence — and a precise map of what would
   take it further.**
   *(Most policy-facing. Best for an MSDE or ministry audience.)*

---

## 30-second pitch

> "India sets skilling targets using labour-market data spread across six official sources that cannot be
> joined — five vintages spanning fourteen years, two occupation schemes, and not one source carrying a
> district-by-occupation vacancy count.
>
> **LMIS standardises them.** Twenty-six official sources under a licence gate, into an immutable checksummed
> store, conformed onto NCO-2015, LGD and NIC-2008 through official confidence-scored mappings — and published
> so that every number carries its evidence status, confidence, coverage, vintage and source document.
>
> And we did the harder part. The problem statement asks for a demand–supply gap. **We proved it is not
> identifiable from the evidence that exists**, so instead of a number that would restate an assumption, we
> publish the finding and the one dataset that would change it."

*(~95 words · ~30 seconds at presentation pace)*

## 60-second pitch

> "Before a state sanctions training seats, it needs to know which district-and-trade combinations are
> heading for shortage or oversupply. Today that judgement is last year's targets plus a bit — because the
> evidence sits in six official sources that cannot be joined. Five vintages across fourteen years, two
> occupation classifications, three different statistical natures, and **no source carrying a
> district-by-occupation vacancy count.**
>
> **LMIS is the platform that makes them comparable.** Sources are registered under a licence gate — nothing
> is acquired unless registered — and stored immutably with sha256 manifests, so results survive a source
> going offline. We conform them onto NCO-2015, LGD geography and NIC-2008 using the **official**
> concordances, and every mapping carries its authority, method and confidence. Fourteen of fourteen
> reconciliation checks match each source's own published totals exactly.
>
> What we publish: observed NCS demand by state and sector, an estimated national occupation composition,
> and relative demand allocation signals at district and district-by-occupation level — each labelled
> OBSERVED, ESTIMATED or SUPPORTING, with its confidence and coverage attached. Thirty API routes, eight
> dashboard pages, 479 tests, and one command rebuilds everything from raw bytes.
>
> **Then the part that takes more discipline than building it.** The PS asks for a gap. Supply by state and
> trade is a joint distribution, and we hold only its margins — 21.8% of the column margins, from a different
> scheme. Margins never determine an interior. So a gap number was available to us, and we declined it: the
> error would have been invisible, systematically wrong in one direction, and it informs real sanctioning
> money.
>
> Instead the system publishes sixteen blocked capabilities, each with a reason and an objective gate — and
> one specific ask: **publish state-by-trade training outcomes with complete marginals, and this platform
> computes the gap with no formula change.**"

*(~290 words · ~60 seconds at presentation pace)*

## Positioning notes

**Lead with the fragmentation**, not the architecture. The judge cares that six sources can't be joined, not
that we used DuckDB.

**State the limitation yourself, early, in your own words.** It converts the single biggest vulnerability
into the most credible thing in the pitch. Said defensively at the end, it reads as a shortfall; said
confidently at the start, it reads as rigour.

**Close on the ask, not the limitation.** "Publish state-by-trade outcomes and this computes the gap" leaves
the listener with something actionable rather than something absent.

**Never use in any pitch:** "forecast", "predict", "skill gap", "shortage", "AI-powered", "supply" (for
training outcomes), "demand ranking", "real-time", "data as of today".
